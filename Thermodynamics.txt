THERMODYNAMICS OF HAIRPINS & DIMERS IN OLIGOOL / STRIDER
==========================================================
A plain-English explanation of the "Ensemble ΔG", "% of Ensemble", and
competition bar (Free / Hairpin / Self-Dimer / Cross-Dimer) added to the
Secondary Structures panel — what they mean, why they were added, and
how to read them. Written as the questions actually came up, with the
real numbers from testing.


WHAT WE ADDED, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
--------------------------------
Before this, the panel only showed the single BEST hairpin/dimer structure
for each oligo (its ΔG), plus 4 runner-up structures. That tells you "if
this exact structure formed, how strong would it be" — but nothing about
whether it actually forms in your tube, at your concentration. We added
three numbers that answer that real question instead of just ranking
structures by strength.


===========================================================================
1) "The tool found a dimer with ΔG -3.85, so why does it say 100% Free?"
===========================================================================

Because "found the best dimer" and "this dimer actually forms" are two
completely different questions.

The ΔG search is a MATH PUZZLE: given two copies of this oligo, what is
the single best possible way they could stick together? It always answers
that question, the same way a chess engine always finds "the best move" —
even in a hopeless position. Best doesn't mean good.

Whether that best dimer actually happens in solution is a separate,
concentration-dependent question. Two separate molecules finding each
other and sticking together always costs some energy — losing the freedom
of moving around independently. That cost is roughly fixed, no matter how
good the eventual pairing is, and it matters much more when the solution
is dilute (fewer molecules bumping into each other). A hairpin never pays
this cost, since it's one molecule folding on itself.

  Example 1: a -3.85 kcal/mol self-dimer -> 100% Free
    A mediocre pairing. Nowhere near strong enough to be worth the
    "finding each other" cost at a typical primer concentration
    (0.25 uM in these examples), so essentially no copies pair up.

  Example 2: a -19.65 kcal/mol self-dimer (a near-perfect 10-base match)
    -> 28% Self-Dimer, 72% Free
    Dramatically stronger, easily worth the cost, so a real chunk of the
    population pairs up even at the same dilute concentration.

Takeaway: raw ΔG tells you how good the best pairing WOULD be. The
competition bar (see #3) tells you whether it's actually worth forming.


===========================================================================
2) "What do 'Ensemble ΔG' and 'X% of Ensemble' mean?"
===========================================================================

Every ΔG shown (IDT ΔG, Strider ΔG) is for ONE specific structure — the
single best alignment the search found. But in reality, two strands (or
one strand folding on itself) can stick together in many different, only
slightly-worse ways too.

  Ensemble ΔG adds up ALL of those ways, not just the best one. It is
  always at least as favorable (equal or more negative) as the single
  best structure, because it includes that structure plus every other
  possibility.

  "X% of Ensemble" tells you how much the best structure dominates the
  others.
    - High % (e.g. 100%) = one clear winning shape. What's drawn is
      basically the whole story.
    - Low % (e.g. 3%) = many different, roughly-equally-likely ways to
      pair up. The picture shown is just one of several similar options,
      not the definitive answer for "the" structure.

This number is informational, not a verdict — see #5 for why it isn't
color-graded red/yellow/green like ΔG and Tm are.


===========================================================================
3) "What does the Free / Hairpin / Self-Dimer / Cross-Dimer bar mean?"
===========================================================================

This is the number that actually answers "what happens in my tube."

It asks: out of all the copies of this oligo floating in solution, at the
concentration you set, what fraction right now are

  - Free       — just floating around, not doing anything structured
  - Hairpin    — folded back on themselves (a SUBSET of "free" — see
                 note below)
  - Self-Dimer — paired up with another copy of themselves
  - Cross-Dimer — paired up with the OTHER oligo in the pair

This is computed with real statistical mechanics (a mass-action
equilibrium solve, the same style of calculation NUPACK uses), not a
rule of thumb. It is the only place on the panel where concentration
actually enters the math — which is exactly why it can disagree with
what the raw ΔG numbers would suggest (see #1 and #4).

Note on Hairpin vs Free: "Hairpin %" is not a separate slice from "Free
%" — it's the portion OF the free population that happens to be folded
into that specific hairpin shape. The bar subtracts it out so the
segments don't double-count (Free shown = truly open + unstructured
only).

Note on missing segments: a segment only shows if it's above 0.1% of the
population. If Cross-Dimer doesn't appear, it means either (a) it wasn't
computed at all (single-oligo mode, or no pairing was found between the
two oligos), or (b) it WAS computed but is genuinely negligible — in
that case you'll see a small dim "<0.1%" note instead of nothing, so you
can tell the two situations apart.


===========================================================================
4) "A -6.21 kcal/mol cross-dimer looks risky. Why is it <0.1%?"
===========================================================================

This is the single most important, least obvious idea in the whole
feature, so here it is with real numbers from testing:

  Oligo 1 alone already has some standalone stability, just from its own
  weak hairpin-folding possibilities:      ensemble ~ -2.5 kcal/mol
  Oligo 2 alone, same idea:                ensemble ~ -4.5 kcal/mol
  The cross-dimer complex itself:          ensemble ~ -8.76 kcal/mol

The question that actually matters is not "is -8.76 a big negative
number" — it's "how much EXTRA do these two strands gain by finding each
other, compared to what they'd already be doing on their own?"

    extra benefit = -8.76 - (-2.5) - (-4.5) = -1.76 kcal/mol

Only -1.76 kcal/mol of real extra incentive to pair up. That's weak —
nowhere near enough to be worth the cost of two separate molecules
finding each other at a dilute concentration, so essentially none of the
population ends up as a cross-dimer.

Compare that to a case that DID show up strongly: Oligo 2's self-dimer.

    ensemble ~ -17.45,  Oligo 2 alone ~ -4.5
    extra benefit = -17.45 - 2 x (-4.5) = -8.45 kcal/mol

That's a much bigger true driving force, so it really does compete for
population (28% self-dimer, in testing).

The deeper reason: an oligo's OWN internal hairpin stability protects it
from dimerizing with a partner, because "already tied up in its own
fold" directly competes with "available to pair with something else."
Raw ΔG of a complex, looked at alone, can't see this — it has to be
compared against what each strand was already going to do by itself.

So: a scary-looking raw ΔG for a cross-dimer does not automatically mean
real risk. If both partners already have some structure of their own,
much of that ΔG was "already accounted for," and the true extra
incentive to cross-pair can be much smaller than it looks.


===========================================================================
5) Colors — what they mean and why percentage isn't graded
===========================================================================

ALL ΔG values (IDT ΔG, Strider ΔG, Ensemble ΔG, summary ΔG):
    < -6 kcal/mol  ............ RED     (strong / concerning)
    -6 to -2 kcal/mol .......... YELLOW  (moderate)
    >= -2 kcal/mol .............. GREEN   (weak / fine)

Tm values (IDT Tm, Local Tm):
    > 55 C ...................... RED
    40 - 55 C ................... YELLOW
    < 40 C ....................... GREEN

"% of Ensemble": always plain BLUE, never graded red/yellow/green.
  Why: this number isn't measuring "how bad is this," it's measuring
  "how much should you trust the one picture you're looking at." A high
  % means the drawn structure is the only real way this forms — the
  picture is representative. A low % means many different alignments
  are competing and the one drawn is just a sample. Neither is
  inherently good or bad for PCR — that's what ΔG and the competition
  bar are for — so grading it by severity would be misleading.


===========================================================================
QUICK REFERENCE
===========================================================================

  ΔG (any)      < -6 red | -6..-2 yellow | >= -2 green
  Tm            > 55 red | 40..55 yellow | < 40 green
  % of Ensemble always blue, no grading (confidence, not risk)

  Ensemble ΔG    = strength of ALL competing ways to pair up, not just
                   the best one. Always <= the best single structure's ΔG.
  % of Ensemble  = how much the single best structure dominates the rest.
  Competition bar = the only number that accounts for concentration —
                   what fraction of the population is actually free,
                   hairpinned, self-dimerized, or cross-dimerized right
                   now, given the oligo concentration you set.

  Rule of thumb: raw ΔG tells you how good a pairing WOULD be.
                 The competition bar tells you whether it's actually
                 worth it, once concentration and each strand's own
                 competing structure are accounted for.


===========================================================================
A COUPLE OF HONEST CAVEATS
===========================================================================

- The competition bar always uses native SantaLucia 2004 parameters for
  its internal math, regardless of whether the Mathews/SantaLucia toggle
  elsewhere is set to Mathews — this keeps the free/hairpin/self-dimer/
  cross-dimer comparison internally consistent (mixing two different
  parameter sets in one equilibrium calculation would be meaningless).
  The individual ΔG numbers shown elsewhere on the panel do still follow
  the toggle.

- Every competition number is tied to the oligo concentration you set
  (0.25 uM by default). Change the concentration and the split changes —
  that's the point, not a bug. A borderline dimer at PCR primer
  concentrations can become a real problem at higher (e.g. hybridization
  or ligation) concentrations.
